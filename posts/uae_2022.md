A bit of a different trip this time. I’ve been working in software and automation development for a while, but I finally decided to make the full transition from mechanical engineering-based software development to a broader software engineering career. Luckily, I got accepted onto a software engineering course backed by Imperial College London. The catch? I found out *after* booking this trip.

So, for the first time ever, this wasn’t just a holiday. It was a holiday with classes.

## Getting There

Flew with Swiss Air and had a layover in Zurich. Fun fact: Zurich Airport has a flight viewing deck with binoculars, so you can watch planes take off and land. A nice touch while waiting for the next flight.

Landed in Dubai at 2 AM. I don’t know what it is about flights to and from the Middle East, but they always seem to happen at the most inconvenient hours.

## First Impressions

After catching up on some much-needed sleep, we spent the first evening at Global Village. It’s a huge outdoor festival where each country or continent gets its own area with food, shopping and entertainment. Two things stood out: the best mango sticky rice I’ve ever had outside of Thailand, and a Bosnian kebab stall that had a queue for good reason.

## Balancing Travel and Study

The next day my software engineering course officially started. The requirement? Stay online for a daily lecture for the first two weeks, then weekly sessions for six months, plus assignments and exams. Finding a café with good WiFi in the Middle East is surprisingly easy, so that helped.

<img src="/cafe_lecture.jpeg" class="image fit">

Later we explored Dubai Mall, which was nice, and they even have a light show similar to the one in Singapore. If you take the metro there, leave early. It isn’t 24/7, and if you miss it the taxis will take advantage of you.

For dinner we tried P.F. Chang’s. Not exactly local cuisine, but the Dynamite Shrimp was worth it.

<img src="/pf_chang.jpeg" class="image fit">

## Beach, Burgers and the Marina

Started the next day at Marina Beach, but first a quick 1:1 call with a course tutor at a nearby café to clear up some learning hour requirements. Breakfast was Nutella muffins and coffee.

Then the beach, where my shoes turned out to be very much the wrong choice. Sand and bad footwear don’t mix.

Lunch was at Salt, decent burgers but a bit pricey, and we walked along the marina afterwards. Honestly, I could happily live on a marina. They always look amazing, at least in the Middle East.

<img src="/dubai_marina.jpeg" class="image fit">

## Abu Dhabi

We rented a car and drove to Abu Dhabi for a few days. Once outside Dubai the roads were smooth and the drive was actually relaxing. Abu Dhabi has a much quieter feel to it. The malls, though? Confusing.

## Desert Safari and a Connectivity Fail

Next day we had a desert safari booked. Let me tell you, getting online for a lecture in the middle of the desert is not happening. I don’t know what I was thinking.

The safari itself was camel riding and dune bashing, and the dune bashing could have been longer. Back in Abu Dhabi we took a scenic walk down The Corniche, which would be perfect for jogging or cycling.

<img src="/abu_dhabi_corniche.jpeg" class="image fit">

## The Grand Mosque and the Sweetest Surprise

We visited Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque and it did *not* disappoint. An architectural masterpiece.

Then it was time for another lecture, so we stopped by Parker’s, a café we’d been meaning to try. Their Matilda cake, inspired by *that* scene in *Matilda*, was ridiculously good, with chocolate poured all over it.

Later that night we checked out Emirates Palace. Finding parking was a challenge. Inside, we were surprised to find the largest Christmas tree we’d ever seen.

<img src="/abu_dhabi_mosque.jpeg" class="image fit">

## The Last Few Stops

On our last day in Abu Dhabi we made a quick stop at Yas Island before driving back to Dubai.

Apparently buying Apple products in the Middle East is cheaper than in the UK, though still not as cheap as in the US. We looked at some phones but didn’t end up buying anything.

Back in Dubai we returned the rental car, which we probably should have done *before* exploring the Gold Souks area. The traffic was intense, but at least the metro was nearby.

Dinner was at Dubai Creek, a beautiful riverside spot designed to look like an old town. We ordered seafood and the portions were huge. If you ever go, order less than you think you need.

<img src="/dubai_old.jpeg" class="image fit">

## The Journey Home, via Switzerland

On the way back we had a layover in Switzerland, which was a shock to the system temperature-wise. We didn’t even have coats. We stayed indoors as much as we could, but still managed a museum, a hot chocolate, and fondue, which is supposedly fine for the lactose intolerant, not that it’s an issue for us.

<img src="/fondu.jpeg" class="image fit">

Would I juggle coursework and travel again? Maybe. But next time I’ll rethink my internet plans before heading into the desert.
